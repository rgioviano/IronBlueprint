# Van Westendorp pricing check

Source: Alex Hormozi, "How I Price Every New Offer" (YouTube short r-9RpAv1P9A). He runs this for most new offers. Pricing is the strongest profit lever, and copying the average competitor price copies businesses that are often broke too. Never set a price by averaging competitors.

## Steps
1. Define the offer in one or two sentences: what they get, how often, how long, and who it is for. Every respondent answers about the same offer.
2. Ask real customers or prospects the four questions, with open number answers:
   - Too expensive: at what price would this be so expensive you would not even consider it?
   - Too cheap: at what price would this be so cheap you would doubt it could be any good?
   - Expensive but acceptable: at what price would it be right at the edge, where you would have to really think about it but would still buy?
   - Bargain: at what price would it be a good deal?
3. Collect customer stats with every answer (income band, current client or prospect, segment such as youth athlete parent or adult, sport, lead source). Without them you cannot slice results.
4. Build cumulative curves for too cheap, bargain, expensive, and too expensive across prices. Read the acceptable range (from where too cheap crosses expensive to where too expensive crosses bargain), the optimal price point (too cheap crosses too expensive), and the indifference point (bargain crosses expensive).
5. Slice by segment: rerun the curves for each segment, such as higher-income vs lower-income. Different segments can justify different tiers.
6. Recommend a price, usually near the top of the target segment's acceptable range with enough value stacked to stay out of too expensive. State sample size and flag when it is too small to trust (aim for 20+ per segment).
7. Report the offer, sample and segments, acceptable range and key points per segment, the recommended price or tiers, and what to test next.

## Notes
- With no survey answers yet, draft the survey and who to send it to. Never invent the numbers.
- If the price lands above the acceptable range, pair this with value-stacking (grand slam offer) work.
- Any customer outreach is a draft for the owner to approve before it goes out.
